Closing the clock-phase gap — sub-µs cross-node time without the hardware feature

A slotted MAC is only collision-free if nodes agree on where a slot begins. That agreement was capped by one thing — the master's build→air transmit latency. Here is the gap, the exploit that closes it with only the RX timestamps a commodity radio already gives, and what it earns in delivered packets — all measured over the production time & PHY code.

One facet of the MAC — validated in sim over real Timekeeper / NetworkTime / RadioBus

01The gap, and the exploit that closes it

Common-view precision is "bounded by the master's build→air TX latency." A build-stamped beacon is stale by that latency at reception, biasing every offset estimate. Two fixes remove it — and the winning one needs no TX-timestamp feature.

02Multi-hop — mechanism, not limit

The first multi-hop run showed a horizon at 3 hops. It was the wrong mechanism: the Timekeeper's uncertainty fusion (great for a mesh) doesn't propagate a chain. NetworkTime's explicit stratum composition does — with no horizon.

05What this doesn't yet settle

  • Adaptive engage/disengage is undesigned. This proves slotting can be tight; the controller that decides when to impose a schedule vs stay work-conserving (the LBT/EDCCA/#111 lesson) is the next piece — capability-based, with a floor, fusing sensors.
  • The residual is modeled as a per-beacon bias driving the real estimators. Faithful for the residual floor; a full physical-layer beacon model (real preamble correlation, CSI) is the honest next fidelity step.
  • On-air. The RX-stamp floor (~0.4 µs, #74) and the shared-reference cancellation are measured single-node; the cross-node closure over ambient beacons is not yet on real radios.
