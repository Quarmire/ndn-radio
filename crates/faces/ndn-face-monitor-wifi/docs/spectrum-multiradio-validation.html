The named channel and the pool — coexisting by avoidance, covered by a team

A name computes its own channel, so peers meet with no coordinator. But the 16 ms Wi-Fi retune tax means you cover many channels with many radios, not fast hopping — and a lone radio can't sense enough to avoid interference alone. The design: static name→channel, a shared occupancy map, and a radio pool where rich nodes sense-and-share so single-radio nodes can free-ride.

Two coupled facets — validated in sim; FHSS not yet on air

05What this doesn't settle

  • Cold-start / blind rendezvous — meeting on an unknown name or with unsynced clocks needs a guaranteed-TTR hopping sequence (jump-stay/quorum) or a self-announcing discovery channel. Not yet designed in.
  • Four unactuated code gaps — FHSS and occupancy-avoidance are disjoint (§9.1); occupancy is single-channel-sensed (§9.2); the static channel is inert in the slot key (§9.3); cooperative reports are published but not consumed (§9.5).
  • On-air. FHSS is sim-only ("nothing checked against a spectrum capture"); the design's avoidance and pooling are validated in sim, not on real radios.
